# Ankara PERT — Logo & Identity Brief

> Paste this whole file into ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude, or hand it to a designer.
> Ready-to-paste generation prompts are in **Part 2** at the bottom.

---

## 1. The business

**Ankara PERT** (ankarapert.com.tr) buys damaged, written-off (*pert*), accident-damaged
(*kazalı/hasarlı*) and scrap (*hurda*) vehicles for cash across Turkey. We collect the
vehicle ourselves and pay the same day.

Verified promises the brand makes:

- 30 dakikada nakit teklif — cash offer in 30 minutes
- Aynı gün ödeme — same-day payment
- Çekici masrafı bize ait — we pay for recovery/towing
- Noter masrafı bize ait — we pay the notary fee
- 15+ yıl tecrübe — 15+ years of experience

**The mark's job:** make this read as *a company you would hand a vehicle title to*.
Not a scrapyard. Not a used-car lot.

**The name and domain do not change.** Only the visual identity is being rebranded.

---

## 2. Hard constraints — design for the smallest use

Four of the five most-used placements are small. Most car-logo ideas die below 32px.

| Placement | Size | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Browser favicon | 24px | square |
| WhatsApp Business avatar | ~60px | **circular crop** |
| Google Business Profile tile | ~100px | square |
| Single-colour print (notary docs, receipts, stamp) | any | 1 colour only |
| Site header | ~48px tall | horizontal lockup |

**The test that settles arguments:** print any candidate at 24px and look at it from arm's
length. If you can't tell it apart from the other candidates, it fails — no matter how good
it looks at full size.

---

## 3. Positioning — three colours are already taken, by our own sites

We own five sites. Three have already claimed a colour and a mark. The new identity must be
distinct from competitors **and** from our own portfolio.

| Site | Primary | Secondary | Mark | Typeface |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hasarlıege | `#0e6b65` teal | gold | wave | Plus Jakarta Sans |
| hasarliakdeniz | `#174c88` blue | gold | anchor | Sora |
| hasarliaracalan | `#7a2432` burgundy | gold | — | Manrope |
| **Ankara PERT** | **charcoal + orange** | — | **open** | Figtree |

All three siblings pair a colour with **gold**. Ankara PERT already leans orange — the one
warm accent none of them use.

**Decision: commit fully to orange, and pair it with a deep neutral rather than a second
hue.** No blue (akdeniz owns it). No gold (all three siblings use it). Charcoal reads
industrial and serious, which suits wrecked vehicles better than a corporate blue, and it
never competes with the green WhatsApp button.

---

## 4. Palette

Exactly two brand colours plus neutrals.

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Graphite** (primary) | `#16191B` | Wordmark, badge ground, footer, headings |
| **Flare** (action) | `#F26A1B` | Buttons, the mark's accent — nothing decorative |
| Flare Deep | `#B5470D` | Orange as *text* on light backgrounds (7.2:1, passes AA) |
| Paper | `#FAF8F6` | Page ground — warm, not blue-grey |
| Muted | `#6B6560` | Secondary text (5.1:1 on paper) |
| WhatsApp | `#16A34A` | WhatsApp button **only** — a platform mark, never a brand colour |

**Rules:**

- **One orange, not two.** Never ship both `#F26A1B` and a coral like `#FF5E3A`.
- **Orange means "do this."** If an element isn't clickable, it isn't orange.
- **The mark uses at most two colours.** No gradients, no third accent inside the logo.
- **Green is semantic**, appearing on the WhatsApp button and nowhere else.
- Never put orange text on orange, or orange on charcoal below 18px.

## 5. Three directions

**Correction to an earlier version of this brief:** "avoid a car" was too blunt. What to avoid
is a *generic intact car* — every competitor draws one, so it cannot make you recognisable.
Signalling **damage** or **recovery** is the opposite: it is the one thing that separates a
buyer of write-offs from a dealership. All three directions below say what the business does.

### A · The Fracture — *recommended*

A car profile split by a fault line. Competitors all draw undamaged cars; drawing a broken one
is instantly readable and instantly different. The crack is the badge colour showing through,
so it stays a strict two-colour mark.

- **Says:** we buy the damaged ones. A customer with a wrecked car recognises themselves in it.
- **Risk:** the crack must stay thick. Below 7 units in the 96 grid it closes up at favicon
  size and you are left with a plain car.

### B · Hook & Car

A tow hook descending onto a car. Says *çekici masrafı bize ait* — we come and collect it,
free — which is one of the most concrete promises the business makes.

- **Says:** we recover it ourselves, at our cost. The most literal description of the service.
- **Risk:** two elements in one badge. The hook ring is the first thing to close up when small.

### C · The Cradle — *the crescent version*

If the crescent is used, this is how: not bolted on beside the mark, but **doing a job**. The
crescent is the flatbed cradling the car, so it reads as Turkish *and* as recovery at once.
One shape, two meanings.

- **Says:** Turkish, and we carry it away.
- **Risk:** most crowded of the three at 24px, and it carries the trademark question in
  section 5b. Read that before committing.

---

## 5b. The crescent — an honest assessment

Using a crescent is understandable for a Turkish business, but there are four reasons to think
carefully, and the first is legal.

1. **Trademark registration risk — the serious one.** TÜRKPATENT can refuse marks containing
   state emblems or flags, and the crescent-and-star is the Turkish flag. A stylised crescent
   that clearly is not the flag is usually acceptable, and many Turkish companies use one — but
   "usually" is not good enough when registering a brand. **Consult a marka vekili (trademark
   attorney) before filing.** This is not legal advice; it is worth twenty minutes of a
   professional's time.

2. **It does not differentiate.** Crescents are very common in Turkish SME logos. A symbol
   meaning "we are Turkish" says nothing in a market where every competitor is also Turkish.

3. **It does not separate this brand from its siblings.** Five Turkish businesses share an
   owner here. If a crescent means "Turkish", it applies equally to ege, akdeniz and
   hasarliaracalan, so it cannot be Ankara PERT's signature.

4. **It costs legibility at 24px.** A crescent is a large curved shape. Beside a car or a hook
   there are two competing forms in a space that barely fits one.

**A better place for it.** Turkishness can be signalled without spending the logo on it: put
*Türkiye geneli hizmet* in the header, a small flag in the footer trust row, and *15+ yıl* and
*noter onaylı* beside it. Those read as local, established and legitimate more concretely than
a crescent, and they leave the mark free to say the thing only it can say — **we buy damaged
cars.**

## 6. Construction rules

| Rule | Spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum size | 24px / 8mm | Below this the badge fills in and the accent disappears |
| Clear space | = badge corner radius | Ties spacing to the mark, so it scales automatically |
| Colourways | 4 | Full colour, all-charcoal, all-white knockout, single-colour print |
| Safe area | circular crop | WhatsApp and Google crop to a circle — nothing critical in corners |
| Wordmark | Figtree 700 | ANKARA light over PERT bold, or set inline |
| Letter-spacing | `-0.02em` | Figtree opens up at display size; tighten or it reads loose |
| Format | **SVG** | Crisp at any size, recolourable, one small file |

### Do NOT do these

- **A generic, undamaged car** — every competitor has one. A *damaged* car is fine and is the point
- **A speed swoosh under the wordmark** — dates the mark to 2005
- **Gradients or drop shadows inside the mark** — break in single-colour print and on a stamp
- **Thin strokes** — anything under ~6 units in a 96 grid vanishes at favicon size
- **A checkered flag, a wrench, or a chequebook** — sector clichés, and none say "we buy"
- **Unkerned Turkish characters** — if the wordmark uses `İ` or `ş`, check them at every size

## 7. Files needed for handover

Current assets have real bugs: `favicon.ico` is **256×140** (not square, so browsers squash
it), `logo.png` is 2MB, `logoq.png` is 3.5MB, and there is no SVG at all — so the site fakes
a white logo with a `brightness-0 invert` CSS filter.

| File | Size | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `logo.svg` | vector | Site header, full-colour lockup |
| `logo-mono.svg` | vector | Footer / knockout on dark |
| `mark.svg` | vector | Badge alone, no wordmark — avatars and tight spaces |
| `favicon.ico` | 32×32 **square** | Browser tab |
| `icon-192.png` | 192×192 | Android home screen, PWA manifest |
| `icon-512.png` | 512×512 | Splash screen, store listings |
| `apple-touch-icon.png` | 180×180 | iOS — needs solid background, iOS ignores transparency |
| `logo-og.png` | 1200×630 | Social sharing card |
| `logo-square.png` | 512×512 | Organization schema — Google rejects non-square logos |

---

## 8. How to judge candidates

Score each draft out of 5, ordered by what costs leads if you get it wrong.

1. **Legible at 24px.** Shrink it. If the accent merges into the ground, stop — nothing else matters.
2. **Distinct from our own sites.** Put it beside the ege wave and the akdeniz anchor. Could a customer confuse them?
3. **Survives one colour.** Convert to pure black. If it collapses, internal contrast is too low.
4. **Works in a circle.** Crop it round for WhatsApp. Did you lose a corner that mattered?
5. **Reads as a buyer, not a yard.** Show someone outside the business and ask what the company does.
6. **Holds next to the orange CTA.** Place it in a header beside a `#F26A1B` button. If they fight, the mark is too colourful.

# Part 2 — Prompts to paste into ChatGPT / Gemini

## Read this first: which tool to use

**For a logo, ask for SVG code, not an image.** This matters more than the prompt itself:

- **Image generators** (DALL·E, Imagen, Midjourney) produce **raster** images. Logos must be
  vector. They also mangle text — a wordmark will come back with misspelled or invented
  letters, and Turkish characters (`İ`, `ş`, `ç`, `ğ`) almost never survive.
- **Asking ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude to write SVG code** gives you a real vector file you can
  open in Illustrator, Figma or Inkscape, recolour, and drop straight into the site.

**Recommended workflow:**

1. Use **Prompt A** below to generate SVG marks. Ask for 5–10 variants.
2. Pick the strongest 2–3, refine with **Prompt C**.
3. Only use an image generator (**Prompt B**) for mood exploration — never for the final file.
4. **Generate the mark only, never the wordmark.** Set the text yourself in Figtree 700. AI
   text in logos is unreliable and this one has Turkish characters.

---

## Prompt A — generate SVG marks *(use this one)*

```
You are a logo designer. Produce SVG code for a logo mark — no explanation, just the SVG.

COMPANY: Ankara PERT. Buys damaged, written-off, accident-damaged and scrap cars for cash
across Turkey. Collects the vehicle and pays the same day. Must read as a serious company
you would hand a vehicle title to — not a scrapyard, not a used-car lot.

DESIGN THE MARK ONLY. No text, no letters, no wordmark.

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS
- viewBox="0 0 96 96"
- Exactly two colours: #16191B (graphite) and #F26A1B (orange). No other colours.
- No gradients, no drop shadows, no filters, no opacity below 0.3
- Any stroke must be at least 6 units wide in the 96 grid
- Must stay legible when rendered at 24x24 pixels
- Nothing critical within 10 units of the corners (it gets cropped to a circle for avatars)
- Must still read when flattened to a single solid colour

MUST NOT CONTAIN
- A generic, undamaged, showroom-style car (every competitor uses one)
- A speed swoosh, a checkered flag, a wrench, a chequebook
- Thin decorative lines
NOTE: a DAMAGED car, a crack, or a tow hook is not only allowed, it is the point. The mark
must communicate that this company buys wrecked vehicles, not that it sells nice ones.

DIRECTION: [pick ONE and delete the others]
(A) THE FRACTURE - a squared badge with rounded corners containing a very simple side-profile
    car, split down the middle by a bold jagged fault line. The crack is a gap in the car
    showing the badge colour behind it, so the whole mark uses only two colours. The crack
    must be at least 7 units wide in the 96 grid so it survives at favicon size.
(B) HOOK AND CAR - a squared badge with rounded corners containing a tow hook above a simple
    side-profile car. The hook is a ring at the top and a J-shaped curve descending toward
    the car roof. Keep both elements bold and well separated.
(C) THE CRADLE - a squared badge with rounded corners containing an upward-opening crescent
    that acts as a flatbed cradling a small simple car resting inside its curve. The crescent
    must read as a recovery bed first and a crescent second. Do NOT add a star, and do not
    reproduce the Turkish flag.

Give me 6 distinct variants of this direction. Vary the proportions, corner radius, weight
and negative space. Output each as a complete standalone <svg> element.
```

## Prompt B — image generator *(mood exploration only)*

```
A minimalist flat vector logo mark for a Turkish company that buys damaged and written-off
cars for cash. Geometric badge with rounded corners. Only two colours: near-black charcoal
#16191B and orange #F26A1B. Bold simple symbol centred in the badge showing
[a cracked car / a tow hook above a car / a crescent cradling a car]. Flat design, no gradient, no shadow, no text, no letters,
solid shapes, high contrast, readable at very small size, centred, plain white background,
professional corporate identity.
```

*Note: expect raster output. Use only to explore, then rebuild the winner as SVG.*

## Prompt C — refine a chosen mark

```
Here is an SVG logo mark: [paste the SVG]

Refine it against these criteria and return the improved SVG:
1. It must stay legible at 24x24 pixels — thicken anything that would disappear
2. It must survive being flattened to one solid colour — increase internal contrast if not
3. Nothing important within 10 units of the corners (circular avatar crop)
4. Optically centre it — mathematical centring often looks wrong for asymmetric shapes
5. Keep exactly two colours: #16191B and #F26A1B

Then give me four colourways as separate SVGs:
- Full colour (graphite ground, orange accent)
- All graphite #16191B on transparent
- All white #FFFFFF on transparent (for dark backgrounds)
- Pure black #000000 on transparent (single-colour print)
```

## Prompt D — build the header lockup

```
Take this mark: [paste the SVG]

Create a horizontal logo lockup SVG combining the mark with the wordmark "ANKARA PERT":
- Mark on the left, text on the right
- Font: Figtree, weight 700, letter-spacing -0.02em
- "ANKARA" in graphite #16191B, "PERT" in orange #F26A1B, stacked on two lines
- Vertically centre the text block against the mark
- Gap between mark and text = the mark's corner radius
- Convert the text to paths so the file does not depend on the font being installed
- Total height 96 units; width follows the content
```

## What to check before you commit

Paste your final candidates back and ask:

```
Render each of these SVGs at 24x24, 48x48 and 96x96, and at each size tell me honestly
whether the mark is still distinguishable from the others. Then flatten each to pure black
and tell me which ones collapse.
```

Then apply the six selection criteria in section 8 above.
